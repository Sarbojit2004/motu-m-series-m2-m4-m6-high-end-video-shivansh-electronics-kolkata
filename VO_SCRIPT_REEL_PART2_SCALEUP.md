## Tone: Warm & Trustworthy, shifted slightly toward Precise & Technical-but-Accessible

The same blend as Part 1 — the series has to sound like one voice — but the balance moves. Part 1 was 70% reassurance / 30% spec authority. Part 2 inverts to roughly 40/60.

**Why the shift.** Part 1 already retired the "am I settling?" anxiety. The buyer arriving at Part 2 has a different question, and it is a practical one: *which size do I actually need?* Per the brief, the M4 buyer wants workflow flexibility without repatching, and the M6 buyer is scaling to small ensembles and multi-host setups. Neither is looking for reassurance any more — they're matching hardware to a real session. So this reel is more direct, slightly brisker, and spends its words on what each unit *does*.

The warmth still matters, though, and it does one specific job here: it keeps the M6 from sounding like a promotion. The moment the delivery gets grander as the channel count climbs, the "horizontal continuum" collapses into a quality ladder — the exact failure the brief names. Line 2 and line 15 are the guardrails; keep them level and unhurried.

**Delivery notes**
- **Pace: 145–155 wpm.** A touch quicker than Part 1. Still not rushed.
- **Energy stays flat across M4 → M6.** More channels, not better sound. No lift on "six".
- The music bed opens already at beat level, so come in confident from the first word — no gentle fade-in.
- The CTA close (line 15 onward) is the definitive end of the two-part series. Slow down there. It is the only place in either reel where the delivery should genuinely settle and hold.

Line 16 is the tightest in either script: 25 words in 4.2 s is ≈ 357 wpm if read as prose, which is impossible. It works because the designation is *phrasal* — three chunks with the dashes as breath points, and the recording should be pushed to the front of the window with the tail landing on 88.0. **If it will not fit comfortably, take line 15 down to "You're picking the size, not the quality." and start line 16 at 81.5.** Do not solve it by abbreviating the designation.

## Compliance notes for whoever records this

- **The distributor designation is spoken in full here**, unabbreviated, as the final line — this is the definitive close of the two-part series, so unlike Part 1 it is not left to on-screen text alone. It is also on screen throughout the 10.3 s outro, and again mid-reel at 44 s and 68 s.
- **All three Market Operating Prices** appear on screen in the range card at 74–80 s (₹26,900 / ₹32,900 / ₹55,900). Lines 5 and 13 speak only the M4 and M6 figures, because those are the units this part covers; the M2 figure was spoken in Part 1.
- **Pricing is always "Market Operating Price", incl. GST.** Never "MRP", never a bare "price".
- **No other interface manufacturer** is named, compared to, or implied anywhere in this script.
- **Nothing implies the M6 sounds better than the M2 or M4.** Lines 2, 8 and 15 are the load-bearing ones. Line 8's "the panel gets longer — the specification doesn't" is the single clearest statement of the thesis in either reel; keep its wording exactly.
- **There is no Part 3.** Nothing in this script points forward. Line 16 ends the series.
- Every specification spoken here is marked VERIFIED in the creative brief's Section 4 master table.
