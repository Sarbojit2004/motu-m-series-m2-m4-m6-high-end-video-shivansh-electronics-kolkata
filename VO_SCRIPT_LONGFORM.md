## Tone

**Warm & Trustworthy as the base, with Precise & Technical-but-Accessible carrying every specification.**

This continues the choice made for the companion two-part reel series rather than picking a different profile, because the two formats are one campaign and a tonal shift between them would read as a different product line.

The reasoning: the brief's own customer psychology identifies the core emotion as *the anxiety of settling* — a creator worried their work sounds amateur because of their budget. A purely technical read would talk past that, and a purely cinematic read would sound like it was avoiding the numbers. So the register is a knowledgeable studio peer leaning over a desk: conversational and reassuring in the connective passages, then crisp and exact the moment a verified figure appears. **Every specification is enunciated precisely** — "ESS Sabre32 Ultra", "minus one twenty-nine dBu", "two point five milliseconds" — because the numbers *are* the reassurance.

Long-form has room the reels did not, so this read is slower and leaves more air. Bracketed cues below are direction, not spoken.

**Pace:** ~140 wpm across ~670 words, with deliberate rests at chapter boundaries.

## CHAPTER 2 — THE SHARED ENGINE (0:40 – 1:32)

**[0:40 – 0:50]** *(L05 — ESS Sabre32 Ultra DAC)*
> Start with the converter. The ESS Sabre32 Ultra digital-to-analog converter is not reserved for the largest unit in the range. It is the same part, with the same performance, in all three.

**[0:50 – 0:59]** *(L06 — 120 dB dynamic range)*
> That gives you one hundred and twenty decibels of dynamic range on the main outputs. The distance between the quietest detail the converter resolves and the loudest peak it will take without clipping. Headroom, in other words — so a mix keeps its nuance instead of flattening.

**[0:59 – 1:08]** *(L07 — −129 dBu EIN)*
> The microphone preamps measure minus one hundred and twenty-nine dBu equivalent input noise. That is the noise the preamp itself contributes. Drive a gain-hungry dynamic microphone hard, and the signal path stays clean.

**[1:08 – 1:17]** *(L08 — 2.5 ms latency)*
> Round-trip latency is two point five milliseconds, at ninety-six kilohertz with a thirty-two sample buffer. Fast enough that tracking through a virtual amplifier feels immediate, rather than delayed.

**[1:17 – 1:27]** *(L09 — the LCD reveal)*
> And then there is the screen. A hundred and sixty by a hundred and twenty pixels of full-colour metering, live on the front panel, for every input and output. *[beat]* Gain staging stops being guesswork against a single clip LED, and becomes something you can simply read.

**[1:27 – 1:32]** *(L10 — shared-spec recap)*
> Converter. Dynamic range. Noise floor. Latency. Metering. Identical on every model.

- Pricing is always **"Market Operating Price … including GST"**. The word "MRP" appears nowhere.
- No other audio-interface manufacturer is named or alluded to anywhere in the script.
- **Nothing implies the M2 or M4 sound inferior to the M6.** The M4 is introduced as *"not a better-sounding interface — a wider one"*, and the M6's own section states its reason for existing is *"not a different sound, a different number of things you can capture in one take."* Where a shared specification is restated it is restated as unchanged, which is the point of the narrative.
