# The ten B-rolls

Ten deployments, ten propositions, one set serving both films. Every clip
carries all three interfaces, every clip carries people, and every clip is
built on the real hardware — the units are fed to the generator as identity
references taken from the product photography in this repository, so the panel
layouts are the actual ones and not invented.

What the references establish, and what every prompt therefore has to respect:

| | Front panel | Inputs |
|---|---|---|
| **M2** | 2 combo jacks, 2 gain knobs, 2-channel colour LCD, 1 big monitor knob, 1 headphone out | both on the front |
| **M4** | 2 combo jacks, gain knobs, input-monitor-mix knob, 4-channel colour LCD, 1 big monitor knob, 1 headphone out | 2 front, 2 line in on the rear |
| **M6** | 4 gain knobs with 48V/MON per channel, A/B button, 6-channel colour LCD, 1 big monitor knob, **2 headphone outs** | **all four combo inputs are on the REAR** |

That last row is the one most easily got wrong: nothing plugs into the front of
an M6. Cables run to its back panel.

## Craft rules applied to every prompt

* photographed, not rendered — documentary lighting, real rooms, honest wear
* the hero unit sits **centre frame**, because one 16:9 source has to survive a
  9:16 centre crop for the reel without losing its subject
* gimbal language only: dolly, orbit, push, rack focus — no whip pans, no
  handheld shake
* no text, no logos, no on-screen graphics anywhere in the plate; the entire
  typographic layer is added in Remotion so it stays editable and frame-exact
* people are present and working, framed from behind, in profile, or by their
  hands — the product is the subject, the person is the reason
